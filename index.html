Risk Methodology & Financing Implications

Evidence-based synthesis · not a credit rating

FX Risk · Moderate

Evidence: USD-linked imported merchandise/payables create transaction exposure; FX forwards are used for related obligations. The 2025 FX cash-flow effect was −MXN 2.77B.

Interest-Rate Risk · Moderate

Evidence: Refinitiv shows no conventional bond/loan instruments, while balance-sheet debt is predominantly lease liabilities. Rates still affect lease economics, consumption and future funding conditions.

Inflation / Margin Risk · Moderate

Evidence: gross margin improved through 2025, but EBIT margin moved from 8.30% in 2022 to 7.76% in 2025 and net margin from 5.98% to 4.93%.

Financing Implications

1 · Preserve flexibility

Net Debt/EBITDA remains low at 0.51x, but cash declined while lease liabilities increased. Liquidity and lease commitments should be considered together.

2 · Match the currency exposure

The relevant FX channel is primarily transaction exposure from USD-agreed imports/payables. Short-horizon FX forwards directly address that exposure.

3 · Watch debt-service capacity

Interest coverage remains strong at 8.61x, although it declined from 9.65x in 2022. Incremental financing should be evaluated against this trend.

4 · Protect operating margins

Inflation and peso depreciation can raise merchandise costs. Pricing, sourcing and hedging therefore interact with financing strategy.

Key Takeaways

Scale: FY2025 revenue exceeded MXN 1.0T and EBITDA was MXN 103.28B. Balance sheet: reported debt is largely lease-related rather than conventional bonds/loans. Risk: USD-linked imports make transaction FX exposure more relevant than revenue translation. Country: Mexico's rate and inflation environment is relevant to consumer demand, leases and working capital.
